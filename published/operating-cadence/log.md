# Change log — Operating Cadence

## 2026-09-25

* **Update**: [How the Planning Stack Fits Together](/operating-cadence/how-the-planning-stack-fits.md)
  now opens with what the evidence supports before it begins dismantling anything. The new section
  names the one strongly evidenced mechanic, the modest effect of formal planning, and the one
  definition worth adopting on its merits. It also indexes both folklore statistics and points a
  reader whose system is already failing to the repair page instead.
* **Update**: the monitoring meta-analysis entry now records both reported effect sizes verbatim,
  so the attainment figure quoted in the prose is carried by the provenance record rather than
  standing beside it.
* **Update**: [The Planning Year](/operating-cadence/the-planning-year.md) no longer describes the
  weekly operating review's exclusion of strategic discussion as documented. The accounts are from
  practitioners, and the separation has not been tested.

## 2026-09-24

* **Update**: [The Planning Year](/operating-cadence/the-planning-year.md) corrects its central
  claim. An earlier version said both published planning sequences give strategy work a full
  quarter before the goals it feeds. Only one does. Amazon's own filed account says its process
  concludes early in the new year, for that same year, so it runs into the period it governs. The
  page now reports the disagreement between the two sequences rather than a shared finding, and
  describes the second as the Swedish site of a Japanese-owned group as recorded in a
  single-interview case study.
* **Update**: the same page no longer presents a plan-then-re-baseline sequence as a documented
  company practice. That description comes from insider accounts, and the filed source describes
  continuous revision instead, so it now reads as a recommendation.
* **Update**: the same page no longer attributes a weekly interval to the monitoring evidence. The
  meta-analysis supports more frequent monitoring against less frequent and names no cadence.
* **Update**: [Running It When It Is Already Broken](/operating-cadence/running-it-when-its-broken.md)
  corrects the comparison between goal-difficulty conventions. Seventy percent is the softest of the
  set rather than a midpoint, and it is an expected score where the others are probabilities of
  attainment.
* **Update**: [How the Planning Stack Fits Together](/operating-cadence/how-the-planning-stack-fits.md)
  separates two samples that an earlier version merged. Advertised corporate values were coded
  across the S&P 500; the finding about employee-perceived trustworthiness comes from a different
  set of firms. The page also now records the one exception the authors report.


* **Creation**: new bundle, [Operating Cadence](/operating-cadence/index.md): how an organization
  sets direction and keeps it moving across a year, with every layer graded by what the evidence
  supports, what is inherited convention, and what is folklore with no traceable source.
* **Creation**: [How the Planning Stack Fits Together](/operating-cadence/how-the-planning-stack-fits.md):
  what mission, vision, strategy and goals each actually are, and which layers the evidence supports
  having at all. It records that the two best-known frameworks place mission and vision in opposite
  order, that a systematic review of 2,233 screened documents found mission-and-performance research
  "scarce and inconclusive", that advertised corporate values showed no relationship to performance
  across the S&P 500 while independently measured ones did, and that the widely quoted claim that
  most strategies fail in execution has no obtainable underlying study.
* **Creation**: [The Planning Year](/operating-cadence/the-planning-year.md): when strategy, annual
  plans, quarterly goals and the weekly check actually happen, and how much lead time the handoffs
  need. It records that the original argument for a quarterly cycle was about feedback latency and
  named quarterly as the slowest acceptable cadence rather than the prescribed one, that the only two
  organizations publishing a dated sequence both finish strategy work a full quarter before the goals
  it feeds, that one of them re-baselines its plan once the year's largest uncertainty resolves, and
  that the measured cost of an annual budget cycle is well below the figures usually quoted against it.
* **Creation**: [Running It When It Is Already Broken](/operating-cadence/running-it-when-its-broken.md):
  how to diagnose a planning system that has already disappointed people, what to change in week one,
  and what to remove rather than add. It records that the widely used 70% target matches neither
  literature, since the originator prescribed fifty-fifty odds and goal-setting theory defines a
  challenging goal at roughly a 10% chance of attainment, that stretch goals are most seductive to the
  organizations least able to absorb their risk, and that more than half of managers in the best
  available study report being unable to perform the translation a cascade depends on.
