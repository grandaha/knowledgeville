# Research Notes

Everything else in this knowledge base is finished. A page in another bundle has been
researched, cited, argued over by a review panel, and audited before you see it. It states what
is known and expects you to rely on it.

These pages are different, and they say so at the top of each one. A research note is the
current understanding of a question that is still open. The findings in it are real and sourced.
The conclusion is provisional.

Notes exist because some questions are worth capturing before they are worth publishing. A topic
keeps surfacing, the reading turns up something genuinely useful, and the honest state of the
work is "here is what I found, and here is what is still unresolved." Forcing that into a
finished guide would mean pretending to a confidence the evidence does not support.

## What a note guarantees

The apparatus around notes is lighter. The factual standard is not.

- **Every number is cited** to its primary source, the same as any other page here, and lands in
  this bundle's evidence ledger.
- **Every stated fact is verified** against that primary source before the note is published.
- **Uncertainty is named where it exists**, rather than smoothed into confident prose.

A note holds its *conclusion* loosely. It does not state a *fact* loosely. Where the evidence
runs out, the note says so and names the question that would settle it.

## What happens to a note

Notes do not sit here indefinitely.

- **A note that holds up graduates.** When the question settles, it gets rewritten as a proper
  page in the bundle where it belongs, through the full authoring process. What remains here is
  a pointer to it.
- **A note that turns out to be wrong is marked superseded**, in place, with a link to whatever
  replaced it. That record stays. Being wrong in public, dated and traceable, is a reasonable
  thing for a knowledge base to keep.

## How to read one

Treat a note as a well-sourced starting point rather than an answer. The citations are worth
following, the open questions are the genuinely interesting part, and the conclusion may not
survive the next thing that gets published on the topic.
