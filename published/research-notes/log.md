# Change log — Research Notes

## 2026-09-17

* **Update**: the note on shared definitions now carries the evidence that arrived since it was
  written, and one correction against its own argument. A benchmark built from real private
  enterprise warehouses finds the strongest agentic setup reaching 10.8% accuracy, and 30.1% even
  when handed human annotations of every intermediate step: supplying the meaning roughly triples
  accuracy and still leaves most questions wrong. A paired test of schema comments gained 8.7
  points on the most cryptically named database and lost 2.9 on the most intuitive one, which is
  the first published result that tracks the payoff against how much meaning is genuinely missing.
  Two vendor benchmarks are added with their own numbers and their own stake named, including one
  whose entire treatment was a hand-written markdown document pasted into the prompt. The
  correction: the note implied the Ossie standard confines itself to metric definitions, and it
  does not, since an ontology specification and a Palantir import path both exist in the
  repository. That argument now rests on release status, and on the spec having no field for who
  owns a definition or who approved it. This corrects the August entry below, which said that no
  vendor-independent measurement of the payoff appeared to exist. That was already inaccurate when
  written, since the note carried an academic benchmark result at the time -
  [Why Your AI Gives Three Different Answers](/research-notes/why-your-ai-gives-three-different-answers.md)

## 2026-09-15

* **Creation**: where a person should check the work when AI does several steps in a row. Why

## 2026-09-13
